Planar Spirals (*Schneckenlinien*), Volutes & Spatial Helices (Figs. 6–27)
==========================================================================

To draw a true :term:`snail-shell spiral <schneckenlini>` (*Schneckenlini*, :cite:`archimedes:spirals`)
beginning at center :g:`O` (Dürer's ``.a.``) and expanding uniformly outward (Figs. 7 & 17):

1. Describe an outer circle around center :g:`O` of as wide a radius as the spiral is to run, and
   divide its circumference by twelve points into **twelve equal sectors** (`1, 2, 3, ..., 12`),
   placing `12` at the top of the vertical radius :g:`O`–``b`` and numbering counterclockwise.
2. Divide the vertical radius into **twenty-four equal parts** (for two complete revolutions, or
   twelve equal parts for a single revolution), numbering from center :g:`O` outward
   (`1, 2, ..., 24`), and transfer those divisions onto a movable straightedge (*puncktirt
   richtscheyt*).
3. Lay the straightedge from :g:`O` successively to sector `1, 2, ..., 12` around the circle,
   marking radial division :g:`P_6`, :g:`P_12`, :g:`P_18`, :g:`P_24` (`k` on ray `k`), and join
   the numbered stations continuously from point to point.

.. picture:: /_static/figures/book1_spiral_volute.svg
   :caption: Dürer's 12-sector Archimedean *Schneckenlini* (Book I, Figs. 7 & 17) constructed with exact symbolic coordinates in :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})` across two complete revolutions (:g:`O` through :g:`P_6`, :g:`P_12`, :g:`P_18`, :g:`P_24`).

By replacing the uniform radial scale on the straightedge with a **projectively foreshortened
scale** cut by rays from an external eye point across a circular arc (Figs. 8–9), the spiral
windings expand progressively outward rather than remaining equidistant—producing graceful
bishop's crosiers and :person:`Vitruvian <vitruvius>` Ionic column volutes (Fig. 24). And in
**Figure 27** (p. 029), Dürer conceives an **eternal self-similar spiral** (*ein ewige lini*)
halving its spacing on every inward turn and doubling on every outward turn without beginning or
end, grasped by the intellect alone (*das fast allein der verstand*)—anticipating the logarithmic
spiral more than a century before Descartes and Torricelli.
