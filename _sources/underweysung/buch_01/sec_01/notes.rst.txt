Notes — Dedication to Willibald Pirckheimer (1525)
==================================================

1. Humanism, Patronage and the Vernacular Program
-------------------------------------------------

Dürer's opening dedication to Nuremberg patrician and humanist :person:`Willibald Pirckheimer <pirckheimer>` (1470–1530) articulates the core intellectual manifesto of the Northern Renaissance: liberating the German craftsman from unreflective workshop custom (*wie eyn wylder vnbeschnytener bawm*) through the mathematical rigor of :term:`compass <zirckel>` and :term:`straightedge <richtscheyt>` (:term:`die kunst der messung <richtscheyt>`). Written amidst the religious upheavals of 1525, Dürer also mounts a spirited humanist defense of painting against Reformation iconoclasm, insisting that sacred art edifies rather than misleads when governed by rational proportion.
