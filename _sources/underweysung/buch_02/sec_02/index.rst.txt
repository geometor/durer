*Demonstrative* vs. *Mechanice* Regular Polygons & the Rusty Compass (Figs. 9–20)
=================================================================================

Throughout his polygon constructions, :person:`Dürer <durer>` enforces a remarkably modern
epistemological boundary between :term:`demonstrative <demonstrative>` constructions proved with
exact Euclidean rigor and rapid :term:`mechanice <demonstrative>` workshop approximations (*von
behendigkeyt wegen in der arbeyt*):

1. **Hexagon, Equilateral Triangle, Square, 8-gon & 16-gon (Figs. 9–10, 13–14 — Demonstrative)**:
   Stepping an **unchanged compass** (:term:`unverruckter Zirckel <zirckel>`) around a circle of
   center :g:`a` marks six exact vertices (`1..6`), since radius equals chord (*darum das die der
   zirkell vnuerruckt selbs gibt*, Euclid IV.15). Joining alternate vertices `1, 3, 5` inscribes
   the equilateral triangle; perpendicular diameters ``b.c.`` and ``d.e.`` with repeated arc
   bisections yield the square, octagon, and 16-gon.

2. **Ptolemy's Exact Pentagon & Decagon vs. the Workshop Heptagon (Figs. 11 & 15)**:
   In **Figure 15**, Dürer presents :person:`Ptolemy's <ptolemy>` *Almagest* I.10 construction
   inside circle :g:`a` with horizontal diameter :g:`b`–:g:`c` and upper pole :g:`d`: bisecting
   radius :g:`a`–:g:`c` at :g:`e` (explicitly corrected by Dürer in his 1525 *Errata*, p. 182!) and
   swinging arc ``e.d.`` down onto diameter :g:`b`–:g:`c` at ``f`` produces both the **exact
   regular decagon side** ``f.a.`` and the **exact regular pentagon side** ``f.d.`` over
   $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$. On the very same diagram (and earlier in Fig. 11), Dürer notes that the
   vertical half-chord erected from midpoint :g:`e` up to the circle—equivalently half the side of
   the inscribed equilateral triangle, $s_7 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}R$—steps around the circle seven
   times (:g:`W_0`, :g:`W_1`, :g:`W_2`, ..., :g:`W_6`) **mechanice** (*so hast du ein sibenteil
   des zirckels Mechanice*), generating the 14-gon and 28-gon by arc bisection (Fig. 12).

3. **Single-Aperture ("Rusty Compass") Pentagon (Fig. 16)**:
   Immediately after the *demonstrative* pentagon, Dürer teaches how to construct an equilateral
   pentagon **with an unchanged compass opening** (*ein fünfeck auß vnuerruckten zirckel zuᵒ
   machenn*, inherited from :person:`Abū al-Wafāʾ <abu-l-wafa>` :cite:`abulwafa:craftsmen` and
   :person:`Matthäus Roriczer's <roritzer>` *Geometria Deutsch* :cite:`roritzer:1487`):

   - Draw two unit circles through each other's centers :g:`a` and :g:`b`; segment :g:`a`–:g:`b` is
     the base of the pentagon, and their :term:`vesica <fischblosen>` intersections :g:`c` (top)
     and :g:`d` (bottom) define perpendicular axis :g:`c`–:g:`d`.
   - Keeping the compass **unchanged**, place one foot at :g:`d` and sweep an arc through :g:`a`
     and :g:`b`, cutting the two circles at :g:`e` and :g:`f` and cutting vertical axis
     :g:`c`–:g:`d` at :g:`g`.
   - Project straight rays from :g:`e` through :g:`g` to cut the right circle at :g:`h`, and from
     :g:`f` through :g:`g` to cut the left circle at :g:`i`; finally, step the same unchanged unit
     compass from :g:`i` and :g:`h` to meet at apex :g:`k` (contrasted with true Euclidean vertices
     :g:`H_phi`, :g:`I_phi`, :g:`K_phi` governed by golden point :g:`phi`).

   :caption: Exact symbolic overlay of Dürer's fixed-aperture ("rusty compass") pentagon (:g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`h`, :g:`k`, :g:`i`, solid gold, base angle $108.3661^\circ$) against Euclid and Ptolemy's $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$ regular pentagon (:g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`H_phi`, :g:`K_phi`, :g:`I_phi`, dashed blue, $108^\circ$, constructed from golden section point :g:`phi`).

4. **Exact 15-gon, Workshop 9-, 11-, and 13-gons & Arc Trisection (Figs. 17–20)**:

   - **15-gon (Fig. 17 — Demonstrative)**: Subtracting the pentagon arc ($72^\circ$) from the
     equilateral triangle arc ($120^\circ$) leaves $48^\circ$, whose bisection yields $24^\circ =
     \frac{360^\circ}{15}$ (Euclid IV.16).
   - **Nonagon (Fig. 18 — Mechanice)**: Three radial :term:`Fischblosen <fischblosen>` (*fish
     bladders*) are inscribed in a circle; trisecting the axis ``b.a.`` of the upper lens and
     drawing a perpendicular cross-chord ``e.f.`` through the inner division gives a 9-gon chord
     accurate to $+0.16\%$.
   - **Hendecagon & Tridecagon (Fig. 19 — Explicitly Mechanice)**: For an **11-gon**, take a
     quarter-diameter ($\frac{1}{2}R$) and lengthen it by one-eighth of itself ($s_{11} =
     \frac{9}{16}R$, *"also das es sich Mechanice / aber nit demonstratiue findet"*); for a
     **13-gon**, take half the radius ($s_{13} = \frac{1}{2}R$, *"ist aber auch mechanice vnd nit
     demonstratiue"*).
   - **Circular Arc Trisection (Fig. 20)**: Trisecting chord ``a.b.`` combined with an iterative
     compass refinement cuts any circular arc into three nearly indistinguishable parts (*"wer es
     will geneuer haben / der such es demonstratiue"*).
