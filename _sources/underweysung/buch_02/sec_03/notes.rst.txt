Notes — Gothic Tracery, Planar Tilings & Pentagonal Parquetries (Figs. 21–27)
==============================================================================

1. Fivefold Pentagonal Rosettes (*Fünf Rosen*) & Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi*
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dürer's four assemblies of regular pentagons in Book II, Fig. 24 (p. 066)—including antiparallel strips and fivefold rosettes (*fünf rosen van den fünf ecken*) separated by golden rhombic voids—mark the historical birth of non-periodic fivefold planar parquetries. Dürer explicitly notes on page 068 that edge-joined rings of pentagons or heptagons cannot tile the plane without gaping or overlapping, directly inspiring :person:`Johannes Kepler's <kepler>` exploration of semiregular and pentagonal tilings in Book II of *Harmonices Mundi* (:cite:`kepler:1619`) and anticipating modern Penrose tilings.
