First Printed Planar Fold-Out Nets for Platonic & Archimedean Solids (Figs. 29–43)
==================================================================================

*The Fourth Little Book.* Among the solid bodies that may be fashioned with :term:`compass <zirckel>`
and :term:`straightedge <richtscheyt>`, besides prismatic columns and tapering cones or pyramids,
there are thirdly **bodies equal on every side in their faces, solid corners, and edges**, which
:person:`Euclid <euclid>` calls :term:`corpora regularia <corpora-regularia>`. He describes five of
them, because there can be no more that may be enclosed in a sphere touching it at every vertex
(:cite:`euclid:elements`, :cite:`plato:timaeus`). Since these are useful for many things, I will
set them forth here, drawn both completely unfolded flat (*gantz offen*) and closed in ground plan
and elevation:

1. **The Five Regular Bodies & the Gore Sphere (pp. 142–147, Figs. 29–34):**

   - **Tetrahedron (Fig. 29):** First is a triangular body having :math:`4` plane equilateral triangular
     faces of equal angles, :math:`4` equal three-sided solid corners, and :math:`6` equal sharp edges.
   - **Octahedron (Fig. 30):** The second body is shaped like a diamond point (*wie ein diamant
     punct*), having :math:`8` equilateral triangular faces, :math:`6` equal four-sided corners, and :math:`12` sharp
     edges.
   - **Icosahedron (Fig. 31):** The third body has :math:`20` equal equilateral triangular faces, :math:`12`
     equal five-sided corners, and :math:`30` equal sharp edges.
   - **Cube / Hexahedron (Fig. 32):** The fourth body is like a four-cornered die (*wie ein
     vierecketer würffel*), having :math:`6` square right-angled plane faces, :math:`8` right three-sided
     corners, and :math:`12` sharp edges.
   - **Dodecahedron (Fig. 33):** The fifth body is made wholly of plane pentagonal faces, having
     :math:`12` flat pentagonal faces, :math:`20` equal three-sided corners, and :math:`30` sharp edges—all of which
     I have drawn hereafter completely opened flat into two joined rosettes, then closed in ground
     plan, and lifted again into elevation.
   - **Meridian Gore Sphere (Fig. 34):** A sphere sliced along its meridians and laid out flat
     acquires the form of a comb (*ein gestal eins kams*).

2. **Folding Instructions & Seven Semi-Regular Bodies (pp. 148–157, Figs. 35–43):**
   There are also many handsome bodies to be made that likewise touch a hollow sphere with all their
   corners, yet have unequal polygonal faces (:person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, :person:`Pappus
   <pappus>` :cite:`pappus:synagoge`). Several of these I shall draw hereafter completely unfolded
   so that anyone may fold them together himself: **whoever wishes to make them should draw them
   larger upon two sheets of paper pasted together (*zwifach gepabt papier*), and with a sharp knife
   score along all the drawn lines through one ply of the paper on one side; and once the whole
   figure is cut free from the remaining paper, fold the body together—it bends willingly along the
   scored lines**:

   - **Truncated Tetrahedron (Fig. 35):** :math:`4` hexagons and :math:`4` triangles (:math:`12` corners, :math:`18` edges).
   - **Truncated Cube (Fig. 36):** :math:`6` octagons and :math:`8` triangles (:math:`24` corners, :math:`36` edges).
   - **Cuboctahedron (Fig. 37):** The third irregular body has :math:`6` square and :math:`8` triangular faces;
     when folded together from its open net, it gains :math:`12` corners and :math:`24` sharp edges.
   - **Truncated Octahedron (Fig. 38):** :math:`8` hexagons and :math:`6` squares (:math:`24` corners, :math:`36` edges).
   - **Rhombicuboctahedron (Fig. 39):** :math:`18` squares and :math:`8` triangles (:math:`24` corners, :math:`48` edges).
   - **Snub Cube (Fig. 40):** :math:`6` squares and :math:`32` equilateral triangles (:math:`24` corners, :math:`60` edges).
   - **Truncated Cuboctahedron (Fig. 41):** :math:`6` octagons, :math:`8` hexagons, and :math:`12` squares (:math:`48`
     corners, :math:`72` edges).
