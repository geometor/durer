Notes — Two Mean Proportionals & Wantzel's Cubic Impossibility
==============================================================

2. Two Mean Proportionals & Wantzel's Cubic Impossibility
=========================================================

In **Plato's mechanical carpenter's square** (Fig. 50, p. 163), setting right-angled legs :math:`gb = g`
and :math:`be = e` along orthogonal axes with right angles at :math:`c = (0, c)` and :math:`d = (-d, 0)` forces
altitude-on-hypotenuse similarity (Euclid VI.8) in :math:`\triangle gcd` and :math:`\triangle cde`:

.. math::

   \frac{g}{c} = \frac{c}{d} = \frac{d}{e} \implies c^2 = gd, \quad d^2 = ce \implies c^3 = g^2 e.

Taking :math:`g = 1` and :math:`e = 2` yields :math:`c = \sqrt[3]{2}` and :math:`d = \sqrt[3]{4}`, so :math:`\operatorname{Vol}(c) / \operatorname{Vol}(g) = c^3 / g^3 = 2` identically! Because :math:`x^3 - 2` is irreducible over
:math:`\mathbb{Q}` of degree :math:`3 \neq 2^k`, :person:`Pierre Laurent Wantzel <wantzel>` (:cite:`wantzel:1837`)
and :person:`Felix Klein <klein>` (:cite:`klein:1895`) proved why all three of Dürer's Book IV
constructions (**Sporus's** graduated ruler, **Plato's** sliding square, and **Hero's** pivoting
straightedge) necessarily employ **neusis or mechanical linkage** beyond unmarked straightedge and
compass.
