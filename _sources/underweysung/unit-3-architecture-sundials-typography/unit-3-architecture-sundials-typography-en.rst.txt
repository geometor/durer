==========================================================================
Book III — Architecture, Nuremberg Sundials, Optical Scaling & Typography
==========================================================================

.. folio:: N1525 p. 077–107

Columns, Entasis, Enveloped-Sphere Helices & Monumental Towers (Figs. 1–19)
===========================================================================

Having treated lines in Book I and plane figures in Book II, :person:`Dürer <durer>` turns in Book
III to **corporeal architectural and monumental forms** generated by carrying planar ground plans
(*nidergedruckte Gründ*) into the third dimension:

1. **Prismatic Shafts, Pyramids & Rib-Vault Piers (pp. 077–081, Figs. 1–4)**:
   Lifting a circle, triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, or compound rotated polygon vertically
   generates cylindrical, prismatic, or helically twisted **columns** (*Seülen*, Fig. 1); drawing
   those same ground plans up to an apex generates upright, right-angled, or oblique **cones and
   pyramids** (*Piramides*, Figs. 2–3). Setting pyramids in due proportion atop columns forms
   towers, while Late Gothic pier cross-sections (*Pfeyler*, Fig. 4) interpenetrate convex shafts
   (*Stäbe*), concave scotias (*Holkelen*), and chamfers (*Fasen*) to spring complex rib vaults.

2. **Praise of Vitruvius & the Six Elementary Molding Profiles (pp. 082–085, Figs. 5–6)**:
   Paying homage to *"the ancient Roman :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>`, who wrote so artfully and
   masterfully in his books concerning the durability, utility, and ornament of buildings
   (:cite:`vitruvius:architectura`), wherefore he is to be followed before others,"* Dürer also
   acknowledges the Northern builder's delight in fresh invention (*"der deütschen gemüt / dann
   gewonlich alle die etwas newes bauwen wöllen / wolten auch geren ein newe fatzon dar zuᵒ
   haben"*). He reduces all lathe-turned and planed moldings to **six geometric primitives**
   (p. 085, Fig. 6):

   - flat fascia (*blatte Fasen*),
   - concave scotia / cavetto (*Holkelen*),
   - convex torus / ovolo (*auspogne Wellen*),
   - salient corner (*Eck*),
   - recessed angle (*Winckel*), and
   - inflected serpentine cyma (:term:`Schlangenlini <schlangenlini>`).

3. **Entasis & the 60-Station Twisted Column of Enveloped Spheres (pp. 086–107, Figs. 7–19)**:
   After two geometric methods for column **entasis** (*Bauch*, swelling at one-third height via a
   three-point arc or stepped ordinates, Fig. 7), Dürer constructs a sinuous **serpentine column**
   (pp. 090–093, Figs. 9–10) by winding its axis through 60 stations on three nested circles in plan
   coupled to a projectively stretched 60-part vertical scale—and determines its true 3D contour by
   conceiving **every station on the curved spine as the center of an inscribed sphere** (*ein
   yeklicher punct in der gewunden axt der seulen ein centrum einer kugel*)! He completes the
   architectural section with three famous triumphal memorials—for a victory, for vanquished
   rebellious peasants (1525), and for a drunkard—alongside a monumental market tower with a
   quadruple helical ramp.

Nuremberg Sundials ($49^\circ$ Pole) & Optical Tower Scaling (Figs. 20–27)
==========================================================================

1. **The Nuremberg Analemma & Truncated-Cube Polyhedral Dial (pp. 108–113, Figs. 20–27)**:
   Dividing quadrant ``b.a.c.`` into $90^\circ$ ($90 = 3 \times 3 \times 2 \times 5$, Fig. 21),
   Dürer sets the **altitude of the celestial pole at Nuremberg to $49^\circ$** (*"also zuᵒ
   Nüremberg ist sein höch neun vnd vierzig grad"*), establishing the right-angled wooden or brass
   gnomonic triangle ``a.e.d.`` containing the polar **Axis mundi** ``a.d.`` ($49^\circ$), the
   vertical **Mauerlini** ``d.e.``, and the perpendicular **Equinoctialis** ``e.f.`` ($41^\circ$).
   Projecting a 24-sector equinoctial wheel (Fig. 22) onto tangent lines ``p.q.`` and ``r.s.`` and
   transferring radii ``d.e.`` and ``a.e.`` constructs both the **vertical South wall dial** (*an
   die aufrecht mauer*) and the **horizontal dial** (*auf den orizon*, Fig. 23), culminating in an
   octahedrally chamfered **polyhedral cube sundial** (Figs. 24–25) whose facets face East, West,
   South, North, the $49^\circ$ Arctic/Antarctic poles, and the $41^\circ$ equinoctial plane.

2. **Equal-Visual-Angle Scaling of High Inscriptions (p. 114)**:
   When carving lettering or statues up a high tower wall ``a.b.`` so that the uppermost line
   reads at the **same apparent visual angle** from eye point ``c`` as the lowest line, Dürer
   divides a circular arc ``b.e.`` centered at ``c`` into equal angular increments and projects
   straight sight-rays from ``c`` onto wall ``a.b.``—noting that any parallel rod ``f.g.`` slicing
   the ray pencil is divided in identical projective proportion.

Roman *Antiqua* Capitals (`A`–`Z`) & Modular Gothic *Textura*
=============================================================

Because builders and painters inscribe letters on high walls and monuments, Dürer devotes pages
115–141 to the **first printed northern Renaissance geometric construction of both Roman capitals
(*ein Lateinisch abc*) and Gothic blackletter (*Textur*)**.

Every Roman capital is inscribed in a :term:`rechte Fierung <fierung>` ``a.b.c.d.`` of side $10$
units (with a secondary $9$-unit canon on p. 130), governed by **broad-edged pen contrast**: thick
strokes equal $\frac{1}{10}$ of the square ($w = 1$), and thin strokes equal one-third of the thick
stroke ($t = \frac{1}{3}$):

- **Capital A (p. 115)**: Bisect square :g:`a`–:g:`b`–:g:`d`–:g:`c` by vertical midline :g:`e`–:g:`f`
  and horizontal midline :g:`g`–:g:`h`, and step foot points :g:`i` and :g:`k` inward by one-tenth
  ($1$ unit) from bottom corners :g:`c` and :g:`d`. Draw the thin left leg ($t = \frac{1}{3}$) up
  from :g:`i` and the thick right leg ($w = 1$) down to :g:`k` so top midpoint :g:`e` falls midway
  inside the apex; place the crossbar ($t = \frac{1}{3}$) immediately below midline :g:`g`–:g:`h`,
  enclosing triangular counter apex :g:`V`. Flare the outer feet to corners :g:`c` and :g:`d` with
  tangent :term:`serif arcs <ausschweiffung>` centered at :g:`S_L` and :g:`S_R` of radius
  $\frac{10}{7}$, fillet the inner feet with arcs of diameter $1$ (radius $\frac{1}{2}$), and trim
  the overhanging apex with a concave :term:`Schlangenlini <schlangenlini>`.

.. picture:: ../../_static/figures/book3_roman_letter_a.svg
   :caption: Dürer's compass-and-straightedge construction of Roman *Antiqua* capital **A** inside the $10 \times 10$ square (:g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`d`, :g:`c`, Book III, p. 115), showing $1 : \frac{1}{3}$ stroke contrast, counter apex :g:`V` $= (\frac{14}{3}, \frac{200}{21})$, and tangent serif fillet circles centered at :g:`S_L` and :g:`S_R`.

- **Shifted-Diagonal Capitals O & Q (pp. 124–125)**: Unlike :person:`Luca Pacioli's <pacioli>`
  four-circle construction (:cite:`pacioli:1509`), Dürer constructs **both the outer contour and
  inner counter of O with just two congruent circles**! Draw the $45^\circ$ diagonal :g:`c`–:g:`b`
  (:term:`Ortstrich <ortstrich>`) through square center :g:`i`, mark centers :g:`f` and :g:`g`
  symmetrically astride :g:`i` along :g:`c`–:g:`b` separated by the thick stroke width $|fg| = 1$,
  and draw from each center a circle tangent to two adjacent sides of the square ($r = 5 -
  \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$); only the short thin hairlines near lens intersections :g:`P_1` and :g:`P_2`
  are rounded smoothly by hand. Appending a sweeping tail through corner :g:`d` to tip :g:`h`
  transforms **O** immediately into **Q**.

.. picture:: ../../_static/figures/book3_roman_letters_oq.svg
   :caption: Dürer's two-circle shifted-diagonal Roman **O** and **Q** (Book III, pp. 124–125): congruent circles around :g:`f` and :g:`g` on $45^\circ$ diagonal :g:`c`–:g:`b` ($|fg| = 1$, radius $5 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$), meeting at lens points :g:`P_1`, :g:`P_2`, with **Q**'s tail sweeping through :g:`d` to :g:`h`.

- **Modular Combinatorial Gothic Textura (pp. 136–141)**: For traditional *alte Textur*, Dürer
  deliberately begins not with ``a`` but with **minuscule i** (*"darumb das schier alle pustaben
  darauß gemacht mögen werden"*): stack three unit squares vertically, trisect top and bottom
  edges, and cap the stem with two $45^\circ$-rotated unit **diamond squares** (:term:`Ort-Fierungen
  <fierung>`) set at the front upper and rear lower trisection points. Joining two such ``i``-stems
  corner-to-corner yields **n**, and three yields **m**—automatically producing an exact irrational
  stem pitch of $\sqrt{2}$ and an interior counter width of $\sqrt{2} - 1 \approx 0.414 < 1$,
  explaining algebraically why Dürer observes that *"the white space between the strokes becomes
  narrower than the breadth of a stroke"*!

.. picture:: ../../_static/figures/book3_textura_minuscules.svg
   :caption: Dürer's purely modular *alte Textur* minuscules **i**, **n**, and **m** (Book III, p. 136): stacked unit squares capped by $45^\circ$ *Ort-Fierungen* touching corner-to-corner with exact stem pitch $\sqrt{2}$ and interior counter $\sqrt{2} - 1$.
