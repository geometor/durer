Roman *Antiqua* Capitals (`A`–`Z`) & Modular Gothic *Textura*
=============================================================

For the Latin letters first (*ein Lateinisch abc*), draw for every letter a :term:`true square
<fierung>` ``a.b.c.d.`` in which it is to be enclosed; when drawing the letter within it, make its
thick stroke one-tenth of the square's side length (with a secondary nine-part canon on p. 130),
and make its thin stroke one-third the breadth of the thick stroke—note this rule for every letter
throughout the entire alphabet:

- **Capital A (p. 115):** First construct ``.a.`` thus: label the corners of its square with
  :g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`c`, :g:`d` (do the same for every letter), and divide this square with two
  crossing midlines, the upright :g:`e`–:g:`f` and the horizontal :g:`g`–:g:`h`. Next set two
  points :g:`i` and :g:`k` along the bottom edge ``.c.d.`` one-tenth inward from corners :g:`c` and
  :g:`d`. Draw the thin stroke of the letter upward from :g:`i` to the top of the square, and from
  there draw the thick stroke down again so that the outer edges of both strokes touch points :g:`i`
  and :g:`k`; thus a triangle (:g:`V`) remains in the middle, while top midpoint :g:`e` falls midway
  inside the apex. Next join the legs of ``.a.`` below horizontal midline ``.g.h.`` with a crossbar
  one-third as wide as the thick stroke. Let a circular arc sweep back beyond the top of the square
  from the thick stroke and trim the apex with a :term:`serpentine line <schlangenlini>` whose
  hollow faces the thin stroke. :term:`Flare <ausschweiffung>` both feet outward at the bottom to
  touch corners :g:`c` and :g:`d` with circular arcs (:g:`S_L`, :g:`S_R`) whose radius is
  one-seventh of the square's side, while inside let the inner serifs project two-thirds of a thick
  stroke width, hollowed on both sides with circular arcs whose diameter equals the width of the
  thick stroke.

.. picture:: /_static/figures/book3_roman_letter_a.svg
   :caption: Dürer's compass-and-straightedge construction of Roman *Antiqua* capital **A** inside the $10 \times 10$ square (:g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`d`, :g:`c`, Book III, p. 115), showing $1 : \frac{1}{3}$ stroke contrast, counter apex :g:`V` $= (\frac{14}{3}, \frac{200}{21})$, and tangent serif fillet circles centered at :g:`S_L` and :g:`S_R`.

- **Capitals O & Q (pp. 124–125):** Construct ``.o.`` in its square thus: draw the diagonal
  :term:`diameter <ortstrich>` :g:`c`–:g:`b` across the square and bisect it at midpoint :g:`i`.
  Mark the breadth of the letter's thick stroke with two points :g:`f` and :g:`g` set symmetrically
  on the diagonal astride :g:`i`; take these as two centers, and from each draw a circle touching
  two adjacent sides of the square. Where the two circular arcs cross one another (:g:`P_1`,
  :g:`P_2`), fair the thinner breadth of the stroke smoothly by hand in its true form. Construct
  ``.q.`` in its square in the very same manner as ``.o.``, then take the length ``.c.d.`` with a
  compass and sweep the tail outward from the circular ring, its first curve passing through corner
  :g:`d` to length ``.c.d.`` and curving back upward to the height of ``.c.d.`` at point :g:`h`.

.. picture:: /_static/figures/book3_roman_letters_oq.svg
   :caption: Dürer's two-circle shifted-diagonal Roman **O** and **Q** (Book III, pp. 124–125): congruent circles around :g:`f` and :g:`g` on $45^\circ$ diagonal :g:`c`–:g:`b` ($|fg| = 1$, radius $5 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$), meeting at lens points :g:`P_1`, :g:`P_2`, with **Q**'s tail sweeping through :g:`d` to :g:`h`.

- **The Old Textura: i, n, m (pp. 136–141):** Although one begins writing the alphabet with ``.a.``,
  I shall here for good reason first take ``.i.`` to construct, **because nearly all letters may be
  made from it**, though one must sometimes add or take away. First construct ``.i.`` from true unit
  squares: stack three upright one upon another, and divide both the top edge of the uppermost
  square and the bottom edge of the lowermost square with two points into three equal parts. Then
  set a congruent :term:`corner-wise square <fierung>` (*fierung vber ort*) with its diagonal
  upright and its corner resting at the forward upper trisection point (and likewise at the lower).
  Construct ``.n.`` from two strokes of the letter ``.i.`` set so that their uppermost and lowermost
  diamond corners touch one another—**thus the white space between the strokes becomes narrower than
  the breadth of a stroke**. Likewise construct ``.m.`` from three strokes just as ``.n.`` was made
  from two.

.. picture:: /_static/figures/book3_textura_minuscules.svg
   :caption: Dürer's purely modular *alte Textur* minuscules **i**, **n**, and **m** (Book III, p. 136): stacked unit squares capped by $45^\circ$ *Ort-Fierungen* touching corner-to-corner with exact stem pitch $\sqrt{2}$ and interior counter $\sqrt{2} - 1$.
