*Demonstrative* vs. *Mechanice* Regular Polygons & the Rusty Compass (Figs. 9–20)
=================================================================================

**Hexagon, Equilateral Triangle & Heptagon (p. 057, Figs. 9–11):** First make a six-cornered figure,
because the :term:`unchanged compass <zirckel>` yields it of itself around center :g:`a` (`1..6`,
:cite:`euclid:elements` IV.15); joining alternate vertices `1, 3, 5` gives the equilateral triangle,
and perpendicular diameters ``b.c.`` and ``d.e.`` with repeated bisections yield the square,
octagon, and 16-gon (Figs. 13–14). Now I will make a **heptagon** from the preceding triangle and
its description by a common way used for speed in workshop practice (*von behendigkeyt wegen in der
arbeyt*). I do it thus: I draw a straight line from center :g:`a` to point ``.2.``, which bisects
side ``.1.3.`` of the triangle in the middle; at that midpoint I set ``.b.``, and the length
``.1.b.`` steps seven times around the circle (:g:`W_0`, :g:`W_1`, :g:`W_2`, ..., :g:`W_6`), from
which the 14-gon and 28-gon follow by bisection (Fig. 12).

**Pentagon & Decagon after Ptolemy and Euclid (p. 058, Fig. 15):** Now it is necessary to construct
a **pentagon** inside a circular ring; do it thus (after :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`): from center
:g:`a` draw a circle, and draw a horizontal diameter through center :g:`a`; where it cuts the circle
on both sides set :g:`b` and :g:`c`. Next draw an upright line through center :g:`a` at right
angles, and where it cuts the circle above set :g:`d`. Divide radius ``.a.c.`` at its midpoint with
point :g:`e`, draw a straight line ``.e.d.``, take a compass, place one foot at point :g:`e` and the
other at :g:`d`, and swing an arc down onto the horizontal diameter ``.b.c.``; where it cuts it set
``.f.``, and join ``.f.d.`` by a straight line. This length ``.f.d.`` is the side of a **pentagon**
that steps five times around the circle, while ``.f.a.`` is the side of a **decagon**
(:term:`demonstrative <demonstrative>`). Furthermore, from that same midpoint :g:`e` on ``.a.c.``,
if you carry an upright line upward to the circumference, you have a seventh part of the circle
:term:`mechanice <demonstrative>`.

**Pentagon with an Unchanged Compass (p. 059, Fig. 16):** To construct a pentagon with an
:term:`unchanged compass <zirckel>`, do it thus: draw two circles through one another so that each
circumference passes through the other's center, and join the two centers :g:`a` and :g:`b` with a
straight line; this becomes one side of the pentagon. Where the two circular arcs intersect one
another, set :g:`c` above and :g:`d` below, and draw the straight vertical line ``.c.d.``. Next take
the unchanged compass, set one foot in point :g:`d`, and with the other sweep an arc through both
circles and through both centers :g:`a` and :g:`b`; where the two circular arcs are cut set :g:`e`
and :g:`f`, and where the upright ``.c.d.`` is cut set :g:`g`. Then draw a straight line from ``.e.``
through ``.g.`` all the way out to the right circle and set :g:`h`; next draw another straight line
from ``.f.`` through ``.g.`` out to the left circle and set :g:`i`. Join ``.i.a.`` and ``.h.b.`` by
straight lines, giving three sides of the pentagon, and from ``.i.`` and ``.h.`` let two equal side
lengths meet above at :g:`k`, completing the pentagon (compared below against the Euclidean vertices
:g:`H_phi`, :g:`I_phi`, :g:`K_phi` constructed from :g:`phi`).

**15-gon, Nonagon by Three Fish-Bladders, 11-gon, 13-gon & Arc Trisection (pp. 060–061, Figs. 17–20):**

- **15-gon (Fig. 17 — Demonstrative):** Subtracting the arc of the inscribed pentagon from that of
  the equilateral triangle leaves a remainder whose bisection gives the fifteenth part of the circle
  (:cite:`euclid:elements` IV.16).
- **Nonagon (Fig. 18 — Mechanice):** A nine-cornered figure is to be found through a triangle thus:
  from center ``.a.`` draw a large circle, and within it draw three :term:`fish-bladders <fischblosen>`
  with the unchanged compass; trisect the axis ``b.a.`` of the upper lens and draw a perpendicular
  cross-chord ``e.f.`` through the inner division to obtain the side of the nonagon.
- **11-gon & 13-gon (Fig. 19 — Mechanice):** When I wish quickly to draw an eleven-sided figure in a
  circle, I take a quarter of the circle's diameter and lengthen it by one-eighth of itself, and
  step this length around the circle, where it closes closely—so that it is found *mechanice*,
  though not *demonstrative*. Further, when I must swiftly make a thirteen-sided figure, I draw a
  circle from center ``.a.``, draw a half-diameter ``.a.b.``, bisect it at point ``.d.``, and step
  the length ``.c.d.`` thirteen times around the circle—which is likewise *mechanice* and not
  *demonstrative*.
- **Trisection of a Circular Arc (Fig. 20):** Trisecting chord ``a.b.`` and refining with the
  compass cuts any circular arc into three equal parts for workshop practice; whoever desires it
  more exact, let him seek it *demonstrative*.
