========================
Dramatis Personae
========================

.. _person-abu-l-wafa:

Abū al-Wafāʾ al-Būzjānī (940–998)
=================================
Persian mathematician in Baghdad whose *Book on Those Parts of Geometry Needed by Craftsmen*
systematized single-aperture ("rusty compass") polygon constructions—including the direct
structural antecedent of Dürer's Book II Fig. 16 pentagon and the half-equilateral heptagon.

.. _person-alberti:

Leon Battista Alberti (1404–1472)
=================================
Italian humanist and architect whose *De pictura* (1435) formulated linear perspective as the
planar intersection (*intersegazione*) of the visual pyramid, realized physically in Dürer's
Book IV glass-frame and sighting-thread instruments.

.. _person-andreae:

Hieronymus Andreae / Formschneider (d. 1556)
============================================
Nuremberg master blockcutter and typographer who cut types and woodblocks for Dürer and printed
the enlarged posthumous second edition of *Underweysung der Messung* (1538).

.. _person-apollonius:

Apollonius of Perga (c. 240 – c. 190 BC)
========================================
Author of the *Conics*, whose Greek classification (*ellipsis*, *parabola*, *hyperbola*) Dürer
introduced into German mathematics in Book I (pp. 034–039) as *Eierlini*, *Brennspiegellini*,
and *Gabellini*.

.. _person-archimedes:

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 – c. 212 BC)
===========================================
Greek geometer whose *On Spirals*, *Measurement of a Circle*, and thirteen semi-regular solids
underpin Dürer's Book I spirals, Book II circle quadrature discussion, and Book IV Archimedean
polyhedral nets.

.. _person-camerarius:

Joachim Camerarius (1500–1574)
==============================
Nuremberg humanist scholar and rector whose 1532 Paris Latin translation *Institutionum
Geometricarum Libri Quatuor* carried Dürer's geometric discoveries across learned Europe.

.. _person-cardano:

Gerolamo Cardano (1501–1576)
============================
Italian algebraist who, together with Lodovico Ferrari, proved in *De subtilitate* (1550) that
every Euclidean construction can be carried out with straightedge and a fixed compass opening.

.. _person-durer:

Albrecht Dürer (1471–1528)
==========================
Nuremberg painter, engraver, woodcut master, and mathematician. Combining goldsmith precision,
Italian perspective theory, and Pirckheimer's Greek mathematical library, he created in
*Underweysung der Messung* (1525) the first printed German mathematical treatise, two-view
descriptive geometry, Renaissance geometric typography, and planar polyhedral fold-out nets.

.. _person-euclid:

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BC)
====================================
Honored by Dürer as *der scharpfsinnig Euclides* ("the acute-minded Euclid"). Dürer purchased
Zamberti's 1505 Venice Latin *Elements* in 1507 and built his *demonstrative* proofs on Books I,
II, IV, VI, XI, XII, and XIII.

.. _person-ferrari:

Lodovico Ferrari (1522–1565)
============================
Italian mathematician who solved the quartic equation and established universal constructibility
with a fixed ("rusty") compass opening in response to the Renaissance single-aperture tradition.

.. _person-gauss:

Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777–1855)
================================
Proved in *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* (1801) that a regular $n$-gon is constructible by ruler
and compass only when odd prime factors of $n$ are distinct Fermat primes ($3, 5, 17, 257, 65537$).

.. _person-hero:

Hero of Alexandria (fl. c. 60 AD)
=================================
Greek engineer and geometer whose rectangular *neusis* for two mean proportionals is presented
explicitly by Dürer in Book IV, Fig. 51 (*durch Heronem den Krichen*).

.. _person-kepler:

Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)
===========================
Imperial mathematician who proved the exact symmetry of Dürer's conic ellipse in 1604 and
expanded Dürer's Book II pentagonal tilings and Book IV Archimedean solids in *Harmonices Mundi*
Book II (1619).

.. _person-klein:

Felix Klein (1849–1925)
=======================
Expositor of algebraic impossibility proofs (*Famous Problems of Elementary Geometry*, 1895)
governing cube duplication, heptagon inscription, angle trisection, and circle quadrature.

.. _person-lemoine:

Émile Lemoine (1840–1912)
=========================
Founder of *Géométrographie* (1902), quantifying operational ruler-and-compass complexity.

.. _person-leonardo:

Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519)
=============================
Illustrator of the spatial perspective polyhedra in Luca Pacioli's *Divina Proportione* (1509),
which Dürer re-conceived in Book IV as planar cut-and-fold nets.

.. _person-monge:

Gaspard Monge (1746–1818)
=========================
Codifier of *Géométrie descriptive* (1799), systematizing the coupled orthographic plan-and-elevation
method invented by Dürer in 1525.

.. _person-neudorfer:

Johann Neudörfer the Elder (1497–1563)
======================================
Nuremberg master calligrapher and *Rechenmeister* closely associated with Dürer's typographic work.

.. _person-nicomedes:

Nicomedes (c. 280 – c. 210 BC)
==============================
Discoverer of the conchoid curve and mechanical trammel reconstructed by Dürer as the
*Muschellini* in Book I, Figs. 31–33.

.. _person-pacioli:

Luca Pacioli (c. 1447–1517)
===========================
Author of *Divina Proportione* (1509), uniting golden-section polyhedra and $1:9$ Roman capital
letterforms; key Italian foil to Dürer's 1506 Bologna journey and 1525 *Underweysung*.

.. _person-pappus:

Pappus of Alexandria (c. 290 – c. 350 AD)
=========================================
Author of the *Synagoge*, preserving Archimedes's 13 semi-regular solids, cube-duplication
*neuseis*, and projective cross-ratio invariance.

.. _person-piero:

Piero della Francesca (c. 1415–1492)
====================================
Author of *De prospectiva pingendi* and *Libellus de quinque corporibus regularibus*, foundational
precursor to Renaissance descriptive foreshortening and polyhedral geometry.

.. _person-pirckheimer:

Willibald Pirckheimer (1470–1530)
=================================
Nuremberg patrician humanist, translator of Greek classics, Dürer's closest friend, and dedicatee
of *Underweysung der Messung* (1525).

.. _person-plato:

Plato (c. 428 – c. 348 BC)
==========================
Author of the *Timaeus* on the five cosmic solids; credited in Book IV (pp. 159–162, Fig. 50) with
both the Delian oracle narrative and the sliding carpenter's-square instrument for doubling the cube.

.. _person-ptolemy:

Claudius Ptolemy (c. 100 – c. 170 AD)
=====================================
Author of *Almagest* I.10, source of the compact single-circle *demonstrative* pentagon in
Dürer's Book II, Fig. 15.

.. _person-roritzer:

Matthäus Roriczer (c. 1435–1495)
================================
Regensburg master mason and printer of *Geometria Deutsch* (c. 1487), transmitting medieval
stonemason rules for the single-aperture pentagon, heptagon, and nonagon.

.. _person-sporus:

Sporus of Nicaea (c. 240 – c. 300 AD)
=====================================
Greek mathematician cited explicitly by Dürer in Book IV (*durch Sporum*, Figs. 44–46) for the
two-mean-proportionals *neusis*.

.. _person-thompson:

D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson (1860–1948)
=====================================
Author of *On Growth and Form* (1917/1942), whose Chapter XVII coordinate grids build directly
upon Albrecht Dürer's proportional coordinate transformations.

.. _person-vitruvius:

Marcus Vitruvius Pollio (c. 80 – c. 15 BC)
==========================================
Roman architect praised by Dürer as *der alt Römer Vitruuius* for column orders, Ionic volutes,
sundials, and the Delian altar account.

.. _person-wantzel:

Pierre Laurent Wantzel (1814–1848)
==================================
Proved in 1837 the algebraic impossibility of ruler-and-compass cube duplication, general angle
trisection, and regular $7$-, $9$-, $11$-, and $13$-gons.
