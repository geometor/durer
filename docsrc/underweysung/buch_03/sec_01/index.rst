Columns, Entasis, Enveloped-Sphere Helices & Monumental Towers (Figs. 1–19)
===========================================================================

*The Third Little Book, on Corporeal Things.* Having treated lines in the first book and plane
figures in the second, I now turn to solid architectural forms lifted from planar ground plans
(*nidergedruckte Gründ*):

1. **Columns, Pyramids & Rib-Vault Piers (pp. 077–081, Figs. 1–4):** Raising a circle, triangle,
   square, pentagon, hexagon, or rotated polygon upright produces round, polygonal, or twisted
   **columns** (*Seülen*, Fig. 1); tapering those ground plans to an apex forms upright,
   right-angled, or oblique **cones and pyramids** (*Piramides*, Figs. 2–3). Setting pyramids in
   proportion atop columns builds towers, while pier plans (*Pfeyler*, Fig. 4) join convex shafts
   (*Stäbe*), concave scotias (*Holkelen*), and chamfers (*Fasen*) to carry vaulted ribs.

2. **On Vitruvius & the Six Elementary Molding Profiles (pp. 082–085, Figs. 5–6):** Whenever one
   speaks of whole buildings or their parts, I judge it hidden from no famed architect or master
   builder how artfully and masterfully the ancient Roman :person:`Vitruvius <vitruvius>` wrote in
   his books concerning the durability, utility, and ornament of buildings
   (:cite:`vitruvius:architectura`), wherefore he is to be followed before others and his teaching
   used. Yet since I now undertake to teach young apprentices to fashion a column or two for their
   practice, I consider the German temper—for ordinarily all who wish to build something new desire
   also a new fashion never before seen. And so that you may know what ornaments can be made with
   the plane or lathe (p. 085, Fig. 6), note that there are six distinct elements from which all
   such work is formed:

   - first, a flat fascia (*blatte Fasen*),
   - second, a concave scotia (*Holkelen*),
   - third, a convex wave or torus (*auspogne Wellen*),
   - fourth, a salient corner (*Eck*),
   - fifth, a recessed angle (*Winckel*), and
   - sixth, the :term:`serpentine line <schlangenlini>` (*Schlangenlini*), which one may draw as
     one pleases.

3. **Entasis, the Twisted Column of Enveloped Spheres & Memorial Towers (pp. 086–107, Figs. 7–19):**
   After laying out column **entasis** (*Bauch*, swelling at one-third height, Fig. 7), construct a
   twisted **serpentine column** (pp. 090–093, Figs. 9–10) along a 60-station helical axis thus:
   now imagine and take it to yourself that **every point along the twisted axis of the column is
   the center of a sphere**, and from each such center draw upon the horizontal cross-line passing
   through that point a circular ring equal in thickness to the straight column at that same height,
   doing so through every point of the twisted axis. Thus the column's true thickness is found
   throughout all its curvature; once all these circles are drawn, trace a smooth contour along the
   outside of the circular arcs, and from this emerges the true shape of the column. To these I
   append designs for three commemorative columns—for a victory, for vanquished peasants, and for a
   drunkard—along with a monumental tower with a winding ascent.
