==========================================================================
Summary — Book IV: Polyhedral Nets, Cube Duplication & Perspective
==========================================================================

Structural Overview (SLUB N1525 pp. 142–182, Figs. 29–63 + Errata)
==================================================================

1. **First Printed Planar Fold-Out Nets (pp. 142–157, Figs. 29–43)**:

   - Publishes scored double-paper (*zwifach gepabt papier*) 2D fold-out nets paired with two-view
     ground plans and elevations for all **5 Platonic solids** (Tetrahedron, Octahedron,
     Icosahedron, Cube, and double-rosette Dodecahedron, Figs. 29–33) plus the meridian-gore globe
     comb (Fig. 34).
   - Publishes the first planar nets for **7 Archimedean semi-regular solids** (Truncated
     Tetrahedron, Truncated Cube, Cuboctahedron, Truncated Octahedron, Rhombicuboctahedron, Snub
     Cube, and Truncated Cuboctahedron, Figs. 35–41) and two non-uniform polyhedra (Figs. 42–43).

2. **Delian Cube Duplication & Ballistic Weight Scaling (pp. 157–166, Figs. 44–51)**:

   - Translates the Delian altar problem into German for the first time via two mean proportionals
     ($g : c = c : d = d : e \implies c^3 = g^2 e$) using **Sporus's** movable graduated ruler
     (Figs. 44–46), proportional scaling gauges (Figs. 47–49), **Plato's** sliding carpenter's
     square (Fig. 50, Euclid VI.8), and **Hero's** rectangular *neusis* (Fig. 51, Euclid VI.2, 4,
     15), applied to casting bells and $1\text{–}100\text{ lb}$ cannonballs.

3. **Cube Sciagraphy, Central Perspective & Optical Machines (pp. 166–182, Figs. 52–63)**:

   - Couples plan and elevation light sources (``o, p``) to project the exact cast shadow of a cube
     (Figs. 52–53), defines perspective via the transparent planar section (*durchsichtige
     Abschneydung*, Figs. 54–55), gives both the two-compass plan/elevation transfer method
     (Figs. 56–58) and the direct distance-point / $45^\circ$-diagonal method (Figs. 59–62), and
     illustrates the hinged glass frame and three-thread lute sighting apparatuses (pp. 178–181).
