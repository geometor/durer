Book III: Columns, Sundials & Roman/Gothic Typography¶

Instruction in Measurement (Nuremberg, 1525) — Albrecht Dürer

Having treated lines in Book I and plane figures in Book II, Dürer turns in Book III to corporeal architectural and monumental forms generated by carrying planar ground plans (nidergedruckte Gründ) into the third dimension:

Nuremberg Sundials (PHOTONMATHPLACEHOLDER000023END Pole) & Optical Tower Scaling (Figs. 20–27)

The Nuremberg Analemma & Truncated-Cube Polyhedral Dial (pp. 108–113, Figs. 20–27): Dividing quadrant b.a.c. into $90^\circ$ ($90 = 3 \times 3 \times 2 \times 5$, Fig. 21), Dürer sets the altitude of the celestial pole at Nuremberg to $49^\circ$ (“also zuᵒ Nüremberg ist sein höch neun vnd vierzig grad”), establishing the right-angled wooden or brass gnomonic triangle a.e.d. containing the polar Axis mundi a.d. ($49^\circ$), the vertical Mauerlini d.e., and the perpendicular Equinoctialis e.f. ($41^\circ$). Projecting a 24-sector equinoctial wheel (Fig. 22) onto tangent lines p.q. and r.s. and transferring radii d.e. and a.e. constructs both the vertical South wall dial (an die aufrecht mauer) and the horizontal dial (auf den orizon, Fig. 23), culminating in an octahedrally chamfered polyhedral cube sundial (Figs. 24–25) whose facets face East, West, South, North, the $49^\circ$ Arctic/Antarctic poles, and the $41^\circ$ equinoctial plane.

Roman Antiqua Capitals (A–Z) & Modular Gothic Textura

Because builders and painters inscribe letters on high walls and monuments, Dürer devotes pages 115–141 to the first printed northern Renaissance geometric construction of both Roman capitals (*ein Lateinisch abc*) and Gothic blackletter (*Textur*).
